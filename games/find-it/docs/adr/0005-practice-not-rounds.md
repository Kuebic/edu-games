# Practice, not Rounds, and the Way on the first screen

Amends 0004, and makes Find It the first Game on Practice (the site's ADR 0014). After the first play with a three-year-old, the user found that matching letters to pictures wants going over, not a path: a grown-up picks what to practise, A to E, F to J, or any mix, and it goes round. The Boxes and their Rounds of six are gone, and so are their Done marks, which old saves keep but nothing reads. Practice shows Numbers and Letters as Topics, each with its Ranges (0–10 and 11–20; A–E to U–Z, by fives as before) and All, every item as a one-tap switch, and the Way chips that were in the Grown-up Corner, because the user changes them often and a 3-second hold each time was tedious. Each Topic keeps its own Scope and Way in Find It's slot, and the Ways of old saves carry over. Every item of the Scope comes up once, shuffled, before any comes again and never twice running, so a small Scope still spreads its turns. The other Choices come from the Scope where it has enough, then from the nearest letters or numbers, so a Scope of one letter still has three to choose from. Six Finds end in a Cheer rather than a Round's end, and Practice goes on. We rejected keeping Rounds beside Practice, which gives a grown-up two ways to do one job.
