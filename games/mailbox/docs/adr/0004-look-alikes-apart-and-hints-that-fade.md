# Look-alikes apart, and Hints that fade

Children mix up b, d, p and q until six or seven, and the research advises against putting letters a child confuses side by side while they're being learnt. So a Round never has two Mailboxes, or two Cards on Find's table, that are Look-alikes, as lowercase letters (b d p q, n u, m w…) or as capitals (B D P R, M N W…), unless the grown-up's Scope leaves no other way. Picking just b and d still puts them together: that's the grown-up's choice to make.

For Unlike and Tricky letters, whose lowercase can't be guessed from the capital, the Mailbox shows its lowercase letter faintly beside the capital at first, so the first matches are the same shape, as in errorless learning with a faded prompt (Egeland 1975). A Round with no Miss on that letter fades it; two in a row and it's gone. A Miss brings it back at full. It's saved per letter, so it doesn't start over each visit. Same and Close letters have none, since their lowercase looks like the capital already. The research calls this design untested; it's the first thing to watch in a playtest.
