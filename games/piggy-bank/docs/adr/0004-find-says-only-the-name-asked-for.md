# Find says only the name asked for

At first Find said a coin's name three ways: the pig's Ask, again when the right coin went in, and the wrong coin's own name after a Miss, as corrective contrast ("that's a penny"). The user found it said the names too often, and a wrong coin's name, heard just after a Miss, left the child unsure which coin she had been asked for.

Now the only name Find says is the one asked for: once for each Ask, and again when the pig asks after a Miss. A right coin goes in with a clink and sparkles, and no name. A second Miss lights the right coin and puts it in, silently.

This drops the research's naming after the pick (Horst & Samuelson 2008), which helped two-year-olds keep new words. The child hears the name just before the pick instead, and that fits her better: repeated sounds bore her, and one name at a time can't be mixed up. Sort keeps its Words, because there the name on a Miss is the coin in her hand, never a second name.
