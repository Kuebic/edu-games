# Real photos of the classic designs, at true size

The Money is photos, not drawings: realistic pictures carry over to real things better than cartoons for toddlers. They're one photographer's set, under the same light, so the coins differ only as real ones do. Coins are drawn at their true diameters times one scale, because size and brightness are what a three-year-old tells coins apart by; the quarter is the biggest and the dime the smallest.

The designs are the classic ones: Lincoln with the Lincoln Memorial, Jefferson in profile with Monticello, Roosevelt with the torch, Washington with the eagle. The shield penny, the forward-facing nickel and the 2021 quarter reverse were made by the Mint's contract artists, and the Mint owns their copyright, so Commons doesn't host photos of them. The 2026 one-year designs, with Lincoln or Jefferson on a quarter, would also break the one face, one coin rule a three-year-old is still learning. A child's real nickel may face forward; the grown-up's card sends them to look at one together.

The dollar is the $1 bill, not the rare dollar coin. The law on pictures of bills (18 U.S.C. 504) asks for under 75% or over 150% of real size and one side at a time: the bill is drawn about 3 cm wide on a phone, and heads and tails are separate files, never shown together.
