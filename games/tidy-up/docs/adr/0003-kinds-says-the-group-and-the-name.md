# Kinds says the group, and each Thing's name

Kinds is a third Topic, not a Game of its own: it's the same sort on the same screen, and the research's order of groups fits the Scope, so a grown-up turns on Steps as they turn on colors. The crates say their group's plural Word as a Round starts, because 3-year-olds told the group's plural name sorted animals, clothing and food near perfectly (11.4 of 12) where those shown examples did worse (9.0; Waxman & Gelman 1986). A Thing lands with its group's Word.

Colors say only the color, but here the user asked for what experts recommend, and parents teach group words by tying them to the everyday name ("a dog is an animal"; Callanan 1985). So each Thing's own name is a clip too: a tap on a Thing says it, and after a Miss the Thing says its name, then its group's Word. Landing says only the group, so a Round doesn't say two words for every Thing. That's about a hundred clips: Grendelkhan's where they exist, then Wiktionary's, then other Lingua Libre speakers', each checked by speech recognition, and a Thing with no clean recording is left out (`sounds.test.ts`). Nobody recorded "mammals" alone, so its crate says "mammal".
