# Two-lane Streets keep right; single-lane Streets carry both ways

On a two-lane Street each Lane has a direction and Vehicles keep right, as on real roads. After a turn a Vehicle lands in the correct Lane for its new direction. That makes a right turn cut the corner through one Intersection cell while a left turn crosses the far Lane, so the two turns block in different places. Single-lane Streets let Vehicles face either way, which keeps the early Chapters simple (two cars nose to nose block each other). We rejected "parallel tracks, any direction" because head-on cars sharing a two-lane road looks wrong and the lane after a turn needed an invented rule. We rejected all-one-way Streets because U-turns then depend on sign directions lining up.
